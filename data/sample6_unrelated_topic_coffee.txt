Pour-over coffee is a manual brewing method where hot water is poured over
ground coffee beans sitting in a filter, allowing the water to drip through
into a cup or carafe below. Unlike a drip machine, pour-over gives the
person brewing full control over water temperature, pour speed, and timing,
which is why it is popular among coffee enthusiasts who want to fine-tune
the flavor of each cup.

The typical ratio used is about one gram of coffee for every fifteen to
seventeen grams of water, though this varies by taste and by the specific
beans being used. Water temperature is usually kept between ninety and
ninety six degrees Celsius, since water that is too hot can over-extract the
coffee and produce a bitter taste, while water that is too cool under-
extracts and produces a sour, weak cup.

A common technique is the "bloom," where a small amount of hot water is
poured over the grounds first and allowed to sit for thirty seconds before
continuing the main pour. This lets trapped carbon dioxide escape from
freshly roasted beans, which otherwise interferes with even extraction
during the rest of the brew.
