Training a large language model from scratch, known as pretraining, involves
feeding a neural network massive amounts of text, often trillions of words,
so that it learns statistical patterns of language. This process requires
enormous computing resources, typically thousands of specialized processors
running continuously for weeks or months, and is only feasible for large
organizations with significant infrastructure budgets.

Fine-tuning is a different and much more accessible process. Instead of
training a model from random weights, fine-tuning takes an already pretrained
model and continues training it on a smaller, more specific dataset, adjusting
its behavior for a particular task or domain. Full fine-tuning updates every
parameter in the model, which can still be expensive for larger models, but
becomes practical for small models running on consumer hardware.

A more efficient alternative is a technique called LoRA, short for Low-Rank
Adaptation. Rather than updating all of a model's original weights, LoRA
freezes them and instead trains a small number of additional parameters
inserted into the network. This dramatically reduces the memory and compute
needed for training, making it possible to fine-tune even billion-parameter
models on a single consumer graphics card. LoRA is one of the most common
ways individuals and small teams customize open source language models
without needing large scale infrastructure.
