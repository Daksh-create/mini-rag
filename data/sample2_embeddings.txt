An embedding model converts text into a list of numbers, called a vector, such
that pieces of text with similar meaning end up close together in that
numerical space. For example, the sentences "the cat sat on the mat" and "a
feline rested on the rug" would produce vectors that are close to each other,
even though they share almost no exact words, because a good embedding model
captures meaning rather than just matching words.

Large embedding models such as those with hundreds of millions of parameters
tend to produce higher quality vectors but are slower and require more memory.
For applications that need to run on limited hardware, smaller embedding
models are preferred. Models like all-MiniLM-L6-v2 have only around twenty
two million parameters and produce three hundred eighty four dimensional
vectors, yet still perform well on many retrieval tasks while running quickly
even on a CPU.

Once documents are embedded, they are stored in a vector database or a vector
index. This is a specialized data structure optimized for finding the nearest
vectors to a given query vector very quickly, even among millions of stored
vectors. FAISS, developed by Meta, is a popular library for building such
indexes locally without needing a separate database server, which makes it
convenient for small projects and experiments running on a single laptop.
